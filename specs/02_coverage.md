# Global coverage ledger, not a universal timeline

All-world coverage is a programme with measurable gaps, not a claim about the 27-entity seed. No finite Western period list is accepted as the ontology. A period requires a definition source, cultural/geographic scope, language, uncertain bounds and relationships to alternative definitions. Labels can coexist; names matching does not authorize `sameAs`.

## Expansion matrix

The rows below are research workstreams, NOT date definitions or assertions that an adapter contains all their material.

| Workstream | Collection/authority discovery | Missing local validation |
|---|---|---|
| Prehistoric and archaeological traditions | PeriodO + museum archaeology records | Excavation context, broad dating, anonymous makers, sensitive sites |
| Ancient Egypt, Nubia and the Nile | Met/Cleveland + regional authority research | Indigenous terminology, provenance, local period definitions |
| Ancient West Asia and Mediterranean | Met/Cleveland + PeriodO | Overlapping cultures, historic places, work/object distinctions |
| African traditions, historical and contemporary | Met + Smithsonian candidate + African institutions | Local collections, community naming and permissions; not one Africa period |
| South Asia | Met/Cleveland/V&A + regional institutions | Region/dynasty/religion/style separation and local chronologies |
| Southeast Asia | Museum collections + national repositories to research | Trade/colonial links require direct evidence, not style similarity |
| China, Korea and Japan | Met + ColBase candidate + local repositories | Local-script aliases, dynasties vs movements, regional authority dates |
| Islamic-world traditions | Met/V&A/Cleveland + regional sources | Geography and dynasty overlap; religion is not a single era |
| Europe, medieval through early modern | NGA/NG/Met/Rijks + Getty/PeriodO | Workshop/attribution, patronage, manuscripts, architecture, prints |
| Americas before and after colonization | Museum collections + regional institutions | Living traditions, Indigenous names, collection history, ownership disputes |
| Oceania and Aotearoa | Te Papa candidate + community/regional sources | Sacred/secret objects, cultural protocols and local periodizations |
| Global modern and contemporary practices | AIC/V&A + artist/institution research | Copyright, performance/time-based works, collectives and living artists |

Start with relationship density, not a million isolated records. Each accepted cell records scope, source counts, object counts, relation-type distribution, missing dates, unknown/collective makers, image clearance, unresolved identities and known biases. A cell is unstarted/partial/reviewed, never complete because an API returned zero results. A Western museum holding non-European objects does not replace locally grounded historical interpretation.

## M1 acceptance sample

At least 100 reviewed objects across three independently operated institutions, four region/time contexts and three object/media categories. These are product targets, not current counts. Include anonymous makers, a workshop attribution, two versions with the same title, a multi-author object, a disputed creator, a BCE date, an open date bound, an alternative period definition, restricted imagery and a documented ownership/custody change. A critic draws 20 assertions at random and verifies exact support, not only the curated golden path.

No automatic region/date-to-period assignment. Contemporary-of is a derived candidate with uncertainty, never influence. Unknown nationality is not a filter failure. No statistically meaningful representativeness claim is made from this sample.

## Seed lessons

The Italian example contains three distinct Adoration objects and distinct Filippo/Filippino identities. The Edo example retains the Met's 1615–1868 label, rather than substituting a more familiar definition. Cross-cultural museum links are explicitly collection links; disabling them must not manufacture another historical connection. `birthDate` assertions can disagree without replacing either source.
