# Leerraum product brief

## Product statement

Leerraum is a German-language visual engine for understanding and discovering real art. It begins as an editorial product with unusually crafted art-period experiences and grows into a structured discovery layer built from the same underlying artwork, artist, movement, symbol, and technique records.

## Problem

Existing options split into two unsatisfying categories:

1. museum and encyclopedia databases that are authoritative but expect the visitor to know a name, title, or date;
2. search-driven art blogs that answer isolated queries with interchangeable articles and weak visual storytelling.

People often approach art through a feeling, detail, motif, question, or visual resemblance. They need context without academic gatekeeping, but they also deserve better than flattened summaries or unsupported symbolic claims.

## Product promise

Leerraum should let a visitor enter through one artwork, period, question, symbol, or mood and leave with a coherent mental map of the surrounding art history.

The experience should feel authored. A page about Romanticism should not merely wear a different hero image than a page about the Renaissance; its pacing, visual hierarchy, transitions, and chosen interactions should express the period itself.

## Primary audiences

### Curious non-specialists

People who encounter a work online, in a museum, in school, or in popular culture and want an understandable explanation.

### Search-led learners

Visitors arriving through questions such as `Wanderer über dem Nebelmeer Bedeutung`, `Merkmale der Romantik Kunst`, `Totenkopf Symbolik Malerei`, or `Impressionismus vs Expressionismus`.

### Students and teachers

Users who need structured orientation, comparisons, timelines, citations, and reliable visual examples.

### Visual creators

Illustrators, writers, game developers, and designers looking for historically grounded visual references and openly reusable works.

## Strategic pillars

### 1. Period experiences

Every major period or movement receives a custom-built parent journey with deliberate chapters, visual rhythm, and deep links into its silo.

### 2. Artwork decoders

Important works receive pages that explain what is visible, how the composition works, relevant context, disputed interpretations, and why the work matters.

### 3. Contextual iconography

Symbol pages explain how meanings differ by place, period, genre, and religious or secular context. Leerraum must avoid universal, horoscope-like symbol definitions.

### 4. Connected art history

Pages expose influence, reaction, chronology, teacher-student relationships, shared motifs, and meaningful comparisons. Internal linking is part of the product, not an SEO afterthought.

### 5. Rights-safe discovery

The artwork catalog stores source and rights metadata as first-class fields, enabling later tools such as public-domain search by subject, mood, colour, orientation, or intended use.

## Differentiation

Leerraum does not win by having the largest number of artist biographies. It wins through:

- custom visual narratives for high-value pillar pages;
- structured relationships across pages;
- unusually clear explanations of individual works;
- reliable image provenance;
- reusable interface primitives that make rich pages economical without making them identical;
- a credible path from publication to art-discovery product.

## MVP hypothesis

A single excellent silo can validate the model. The initial pilot should be `Romantik` and include:

- one bespoke period landing experience;
- at least four artwork decoders;
- at least two artist pages;
- at least two symbol or motif pages;
- one technique or concept page;
- one comparison page;
- a period index and homepage entry point;
- complete artwork rights records for every displayed image.

Suggested initial connective pages:

- Caspar David Friedrich;
- William Turner;
- *Der Wanderer über dem Nebelmeer*;
- *Mönch am Meer*;
- *Das Eismeer*;
- *The Fighting Temeraire*;
- the sublime;
- ruins;
- fog and obscured landscapes;
- Romanticism versus Classicism.

## Business model sequence

Revenue is not required for the first product proof. Potential layers should emerge from validated behavior rather than distort the editorial foundation:

1. sponsorships and contextual affiliate relationships for books, prints, exhibitions, or museum travel;
2. premium reference boards and exports for creators;
3. advanced public-domain artwork search and saved collections;
4. educational accounts, assignments, and classroom collections;
5. data or search API for publishers and creative tools.

## Success measures

### Product quality

- readers move from a pillar page into child pages;
- artwork pages receive meaningful scroll depth and interaction;
- users can identify what makes a period visually distinct after reading;
- rights and source fields are complete for every published image.

### Organic discovery

- indexed pages receive non-branded impressions;
- pillar pages rank for period-level queries;
- child pages acquire long-tail queries around meaning, symbolism, comparison, and technique;
- the share of traffic landing on non-homepage routes grows over time.

### Compounding value

- every new artwork can appear in multiple contexts without duplicating records;
- shared components reduce build cost while bespoke compositions remain visibly distinct;
- taxonomies and relationships make later search features possible.

## Non-goals for the MVP

- comprehensive coverage of all art history;
- contemporary art news;
- user accounts;
- a general-purpose CMS;
- AI-generated artwork;
- automatic publication from scraped museum descriptions;
- a global exhibition calendar;
- a production database before static content becomes a genuine constraint.
